The Entry Surface

Carver reads the FDA label and builds every distinct way a molecule can reach the market. Patents, competitor labels, formularies, clinical evidence and spend are all measured on that one surface.

Why the label

Product decisions are made relative to commercial essentiality and market entry opportunities. For FDA-regulated products, the object of these decisions is the product label.

But not just any part of the label. The parts that are absolutely necessary for market entry.

An “Entry Pathway” is the unit of economic viability.

White-Spot Detection for 505(b)(2) Sponsors

505(b)(2) developersContract research organizationsSpecialty generics

Carver detects product gaps on the drug label: uses the label approves for a patient group that no marketed product serves well. Each gap is a candidate for a new form of an existing ingredient, tested against the existing patents use by use.

Pediatric uses with only pillsA use labeled for children with no oral liquid in the ingredient family, graded by the youngest labeled age.
Injectables that need preparationPowders, concentrates and vials with no premixed bag or prefilled syringe on that route.
Multi-dose regimens without extended releaseUses labeled twice or three times daily with no extended-release version.

Method & data

Built to be audited.

Carver’s measurement is deterministic and reproducible. Language models assist at the edges; every number traces back to a label sentence, a patent claim or a public record.

Anchored to the label

The FDA label is the document every party already relies on. Carver’s units come from it, and the rules for generic sameness make them the right units for entry and substitution.

Traceable to the sentence

Each pathway, wall and match carries the label sentence, claim limitation or formulary row it came from, so an analyst or a lawyer can check any number.

Versioned through time

Labels, listings and formularies change. Carver keeps every label edition and dates every patent wall, so each dossier reflects the market on a specific date.

Validated against real entry

Pathways Carver marks as newly open show significantly more generic entry within the same drug and year, including in a natural experiment around the 2012 Caraco v. Novo Nordisk decision on use codes.

Company

Start with one drug.

Carver’s mission is to provide the information technology necessary for efficient decision-making across the pharmaceutical value chain, from drug manufacturer all the way down to the patient.

Providing therapeutically appropriate and cost-effective medications is a complicated process of many interacting parties. At every step a decision is made about the product, yet unclear reporting standards and lagging measurement technology impair those decisions. This is the friction Carver solves.

Carver grew out of academic research on how drug makers disclose patent protection and how that disclosure shapes generic entry. The company builds that measurement into tools for every party that handles a drug on its way to a patient.
